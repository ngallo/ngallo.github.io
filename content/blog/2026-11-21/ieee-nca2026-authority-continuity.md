From November 10 to 13, 2026, the **IEEE 24th International Symposium on Network Computing and Applications (NCA 2026)** took place in Ortigia-Syracuse, Sicily. The program included the paper *“Authority Continuity for Verifiable Lineage in Distributed Systems and AI Agents”*, by Nicola Gallo, Stefano Silvestri and Antonio Radesca (Nitro Agility S.r.l. and ICAR, National Research Council of Italy, CNR).

<!--more-->

If you are interested in authority continuity, the confused deputy problem over time, and verifiable lineage for AI agents, you can find the conference program and the presentation materials here.

**Presentation summary.** Most authorization today is possession-based: if you hold the key, the token or the capability, the door opens. The receiver checks the key, the operation and the resource, but not the execution that brought the request. This talk presents Authority Continuity in PIC (Provenance Identity Continuity): authority can continue only if the executor can prove a causal continuation, in the temporal dimension of the authorization model. Starting from the possession, delegation and safety trade-off, PIC gives up history-blind authorization and requires a Proof of Relationship at every hop, composed into a Proof of Continuity with non-expanding authority. The result is a formal exclusion of the confused deputy over time, even when the executor errs, illustrated with AI agents that hold several requests and continue the wrong one.
